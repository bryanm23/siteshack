The approach

People look you up before they walk in.

They want the hours, a phone number, a sense of the place, and proof you do the work. The site is that answer, sitting there at night when someone is deciding who to call in the morning.

The Site Shack works with small businesses in New Jersey. The pages stay few, in an order a customer can follow on a phone. The words sound like the business, because they come from a conversation with you.

  1. 1
    The offer, first

    Your name, what you do, and the town, plus a way to call or visit before any scrolling.

  2. 2
    Proof of the place

    A few true photos of the work, the shop, or the food. Phone photos are welcome.

  3. 3
    The work itself

    Services or a menu, written the way a customer would look for them.

  4. 4
    The practical bits

    Hours, the area you cover, and where to show up.

Process

From a conversation to a site you can run.

  1. 01

    Listen

    A call or a written note. What you sell, who it’s for, and what should happen when someone lands on the site. You send photos and the details a stranger would need.

  2. 02

    Draft

    A site you can click through, with real words. You mark what sounds wrong. One main round of revisions is included, then the loose ends get tied.

  3. 03

    Launch

    Domain connected, phones checked, contact path tested. You leave with a walkthrough for changing hours, prices, or a photo.

  4. 04

    Look after

    Ordinary updates when the season, menu, or services change. Larger additions are quoted on their own before they start.
