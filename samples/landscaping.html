On the page

What a customer can read before they call.

A landscaping site has to answer the practical questions: what the crew does, how a yard gets on the route, and what you need to tell them about the gate and the dog.

Included work

  • Weekly mow, edge, and blow-off of walks and the drive.
  • Spring cleanup: beds raked, winter debris hauled, first cut of the year.
  • Mulch refresh and a shrub trim so the front reads as finished.
  • Fall leaf rounds and a one-time cleanup after a storm.
  • A written day of the week, so you know when the truck is coming.

How a yard gets on the route

  1. You call or send the address and what the yard needs.
  2. Someone looks at the lot, or you send photos of the front and the back.
  3. You get a plain price: weekly, or a one-time cleanup.
  4. The crew starts on the day you agreed, and skips a week if you leave a note.

What to send

  • The town, and a photo of the front yard.
  • How often you want mowing.
  • Gates, dogs, and anything the mower should stay off.
  • Whether beds and mulch should happen in the same visit.

Before you call

Questions people ask about a lawn route.

Do I have to be home?

No. Leave the gate the way the crew needs it, and a note if the dog is out. A real site can add where to put the key.

What if it rains on my day?

The crew moves the route. A real site would say whether they text the new day, or you just see them the next dry morning.

Can I pause for a vacation?

Yes. Tell them the week the house is empty so they do not mow it for nothing. The page should make that request obvious.
