On the page

A checklist, then a price.

Cleaning sites lose trust when “whole house” means something different every week. This page ties the visit to a list, and marks the jobs that are extra.

What a visit covers

  • Kitchen, baths, floors, and the surfaces you live on.
  • The same checklist every week, or every other week.
  • Move-out cleans: empty rooms, appliances, and baths.
  • Small offices after hours.
  • A price tied to that list, not a vague whole-house line.

How a first clean works

  1. You say the size of the home or the office.
  2. You pick weekly, every other week, or a one-time clean.
  3. They send the checklist and the price.
  4. The first visit runs longer if the house has been waiting.

What to tell them

  • Beds and baths.
  • Pets.
  • Products you do not want used.
  • A door code, or where the key is.

Coverage

Homes and small offices. The towns stay blank.

Before the first visit

Questions the checklist should answer.

Do I need to be home?

No, if they can get in. Say how: a code, a key, or someone who will be there.

Are supplies included?

This sample assumes the cleaner brings supplies. A real page should say if you prefer your own products.

What is not in a standard clean?

Ovens, the inside of the fridge, and inside windows are often extra. The checklist should mark them instead of leaving it to the day of.
